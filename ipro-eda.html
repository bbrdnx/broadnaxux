IPRO

Early Data Assessment

A searchable document repository that gives legal teams access to case files before traditional eDiscovery processing finishes.

About IPRO

IPRO builds e-discovery software that litigation teams rely on as an industry standard. Their platform handles the full lifecycle of electronic evidence (identify, preserve, collect, process, review, produce) for legal cases and investigations.

Role

Product Designer

Domain

Legal Technology / eDiscovery

Focus

Pre-discovery & document repository

The full EDA workflow. Case creation, processing, and review, all in one connected experience.

The Problem

Days of waiting before the real work could begin

In eDiscovery, legal teams follow the EDRM (Electronic Discovery Reference Model), a framework for recovering and reviewing digital data ethically and repeatably. It starts with ingestion: tens of thousands of files imaged, OCR'd, and indexed before anyone can look at them.

Depending on the client's resources, processing could take a week. During that wait, the legal team was locked out of their own case materials.

Just under 75% of IPRO clients expected to wait longer than 48 hours to access documents after ingesting them. Most had accepted it as an industry reality.

Research

What happens during the wait

I spent time with internal service providers and clients running large cases to understand what they did while documents processed. Two patterns emerged.

  • Teams juggled overlapping cases. While one was processing, they worked on others. The waiting case faded from memory, creating costly ramp-up time when documents finally arrived.
  • Larger law firms and corporate legal teams reused many of the same documents across long-running investigations. They already had those files elsewhere, just not through their review software.

The second insight cracked the strategy open. Most corporations store employee and client documents in a cloud with user-based permissions, fed by connections to Microsoft, Google, and other enterprise platforms. What if legal software connected to that same cloud, making every document accessible through the eDiscovery tool?

The EDRM (Electronic Discovery Reference Model). The industry framework IPRO was extending by "moving to the left."

Industry Context

"Moving the EDRM to the left"

This wasn't just IPRO's idea. At legal conventions across North America, industry leaders like Relativity, Summation, and Microsoft were all signaling plans for pre-discovery tools. "Moving the EDRM to the left," enabling work before the traditional processing step, was becoming a competitive imperative.

It was also an extremely sensitive proposition. Legal teams needed absolute confidence in the security and permissioning of any repository touching their case documents. Privacy, chain-of-custody, and client trust all had to be addressed before a single document could surface through a new pathway.

Approach

Building trust through research

I wrote targeted research questions with my team to understand what it would take for clients to trust a document repository built into their eDiscovery tool. Not just the features they wanted, but the guarantees they'd require, and what they'd need to prove to their own clients about the security of the solution.

This was as much a product strategy challenge as a design challenge. The UI had to communicate security and control at every step. In this domain, confidence in the tool is inseparable from the tool's usefulness.

Case creation. The starting point for every new investigation in the EDA workflow.

Repository entry point. Explaining the value and security of the document repository.

Repository settings. Granular controls for security, permissions, and connection management.

Validation success. Confirming to users that their repository connection is secure and working.

Outcome

Shipped, then I moved on

EDA shipped before I left IPRO, so I don't have post-launch metrics to point to. What I can say: it shipped, and it landed in a market where every major eDiscovery player (Relativity, Summation, Microsoft) was racing for the same ground.
