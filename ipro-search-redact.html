About IPRO

IPRO builds e-discovery software that litigation teams rely on as an industry standard. Their platform handles the full lifecycle of electronic evidence (identify, preserve, collect, process, review, produce) for legal cases and investigations.

Role

Product Designer

Domain

Legal Technology / eDiscovery

Focus

Workspace redesign & annotation UX

The redesigned transcript workspace. Annotations, issues, and exhibits, all without losing sight of the document.

The Problem

A modal that blocked the very thing users needed to see

Legal professionals using IPRO's transcript review tool had a fundamental problem. Every time they tagged an issue, linked an exhibit, or added a note, a modal popped up and covered the transcript text. They literally couldn't see what they were working on.

The result was a cycle of read, remember, open the modal, edit, close it, re-find your place. Combined with links opening in new tabs and the exhibit manager living elsewhere, reviewers spent as much energy navigating the UI as doing the work.

The interface had also grown cluttered over time. A single Cancel/OK pattern handled issues, exhibits, and notes. Three distinct actions, one interaction model.

Before. The original workspace with modal-based annotation flow.

After. Side panel approach keeps the transcript visible at all times.

The Solution

From modals to side panels: eyes on the transcript

The core move was replacing a single blocking modal with collapsible side panels. The transcript stayed visible while users annotated, tagged, and linked, eliminating the context switching that had plagued the old experience.

  • A collapsible transcript navigation panel, since users told me they rarely jump between transcripts while annotating.
  • A "Notebook" panel replacing the modal, housing issues, exhibits, and notes with inline editing.
  • Tabs to toggle between the Word Index and Notebook, keeping the layout clean.
  • The ability to tag and link content without ever taking eyes off the transcript text.

Design Detail

Rethinking text highlighting for accessibility

The annotation system relied heavily on colored highlights, but the existing approach was breaking down. Blue underlined links were an accessibility issue. And with multiple features requiring text emphasis (entity highlighting, annotations, redactions, search results), the same visual style was doing the work of four.

I moved underlines to dotted outlines for selected text, making the distinction between issue highlights and links immediately clear. That also left room for future URL linking without visual collision.

For color, I kept highlighting but developed a researched palette of accessible colors that could be reused across IPRO's product suite. Users could also control opacity and contrast, critical for professionals spending hours in dense documents.

Outcome

A workspace that works with users, not against them

The redesigned workspace changed how legal reviewers interacted with transcripts. The document stayed visible at all times. Annotations became a natural extension of reading instead of an interruption, reducing the cognitive overhead of a task that already demands intense focus.

The modular panel system also opened the door to future work (exhibit management, expanded linking, deeper integration with IPRO's broader review tools) without adding another modal to the stack.
